Sensitivity & Response
(Pale Blue) 		Microphone Sensitivity
(5.1) M = e/p (V/Pa)
Sensitivity Characteristics:
• Higher M = more sensitive microphone
• Typical range: 1-100 mV/Pa
• Temperature and humidity dependent
• Frequency dependent for real microphones
Output voltage per unit acoustic pressure. Fundamental specification determining microphone gain requirements and signal-to-noise ratio. Condenser: 20mV/Pa
Dynamic: 2mV/Pa
Ribbon: 1mV/Pa
Instantaneous Response
(5.2) e(t) = M × p(t)
Linear Response Assumption:
• Valid for small signal levels
• Assumes frequency-independent sensitivity
• Real microphones have nonlinearities
• Maximum SPL limitations apply
Time-domain relationship between acoustic pressure and electrical output. Assumes ideal linear transduction. 1 Pa pressure →
20 mV output
(for M=20mV/Pa)
Sensitivity in dB
(5.3) M_dB = 20 log₁₀(M/M_ref)
Standard References:
• IEC Standard: M_ref = 1 V/Pa (0 dB)
• Old Standard: 1 V/μbar = 1 V/0.1Pa
• Typical Range: -60 to -20 dB re 1V/Pa
• Condenser mics: -30 to -40 dB re 1V/Pa
Logarithmic representation of sensitivity relative to standard reference. Industry standard specification format. 20mV/Pa =
-34 dB re 1V/Pa
Electrical Impedance
(5.5) Z_mic = V/I (complex)
Impedance Categories:
• Low-Z: 50-600Ω (professional)
• High-Z: 1-50kΩ (guitar pickups)
• Balanced operation: Low-Z preferred
• Cable loading: Affects frequency response
Complex electrical impedance at microphone terminals. Determines interface requirements and cable effects. Most pro mics:
150-200Ω
balanced XLR
Transduction Types
(Pale Green) 		Dynamic (Moving Coil)
(5.7) e = B × l × v
Dynamic Microphone Characteristics:
• Principle: Electromagnetic induction
• Advantages: Rugged, no power required
• Disadvantages: Lower sensitivity, mass inertia
• Applications: Live sound, drums, guitar amps
Electromagnetic transduction using moving coil in magnetic field. Robust, passive operation with moderate sensitivity. Shure SM57/58
Sennheiser MD421
Electro-Voice RE20
Condenser (Capacitor)
(5.8) e = (Q/C) × (ΔC/C₀) = V_bias × (Δd/d₀)
Condenser Microphone Characteristics:
• Principle: Variable capacitance
• Advantages: High sensitivity, wide frequency response
• Disadvantages: Requires power, humidity sensitive
• Power: Phantom power (48V) or battery
Electrostatic transduction using variable capacitor with polarizing voltage. High sensitivity and excellent frequency response. Neumann U87
AKG C414
Audio-Technica AT4040
Ribbon
(5.9) e = B × w × v_ribbon
Ribbon Microphone Characteristics:
• Principle: Electromagnetic induction (ribbon)
• Advantages: Natural sound, bidirectional
• Disadvantages: Fragile, low output, wind sensitive
• Pattern: Natural figure-8 response
Electromagnetic transduction using thin ribbon conductor. Produces natural sound with inherent bidirectional pattern. Royer R-121
Coles 4038
AEA R84
Electret Condenser
(5.10) e = K × (Δd/d₀) (permanent charge)
Electret Characteristics:
• Principle: Permanently polarized dielectric
• Advantages: No bias voltage, compact size
• Applications: Lavalier, measurement, consumer
• Power: Low voltage for preamp only
Condenser variant using permanently charged electret material. Eliminates need for external polarizing voltage. Measurement mics
Lavalier mics
Phone mics
Directivity Patterns
(Pale Pink) 		Pressure Gradient (Cosine)
(5.4) p(θ) = p₀ × cos(θ)
Cosine Response Analysis:
• 0°: Maximum response (on-axis)
• 90°: Null response (perpendicular)
• 180°: Inverted response (rear)
• Creates natural cardioid pattern
Directional response of pressure-gradient microphone following cosine law. Foundation for cardioid and bidirectional patterns. 90° = -∞ dB
120° = -6 dB
180° = 0 dB inverted
Cardioid Pattern
(5.11) R(θ) = 0.5 × (1 + cos(θ))
Cardioid Characteristics:
• Front response: Maximum sensitivity
• Side response: -6 dB at 90°
• Rear response: Theoretical null at 180°
• Applications: Vocal recording, instrument isolation
Heart-shaped pattern combining pressure and pressure-gradient responses. Most common directional microphone type. Rejects rear sounds
Good isolation
Proximity effect
Omnidirectional
(5.12) R(θ) = 1 (all directions)
Omnidirectional Properties:
• Uniform response: All directions equal
• No proximity effect: Pressure-operated only
• Natural sound: Room acoustics included
• Applications: Ambient recording, measurement
Equal sensitivity in all directions. Pure pressure-operated microphone with no directional discrimination. Room recording
Orchestral sections
Measurement
Bidirectional (Figure-8)
(5.13) R(θ) = cos(θ)
Bidirectional Characteristics:
• Front/rear response: Equal sensitivity
• Side nulls: Perfect rejection at 90°
• Strong proximity effect: Pure gradient operation
• Applications: Mid-side recording, Blumlein pairs
Equal response front and rear with side nulls. Pure pressure-gradient operation providing maximum directional discrimination. Ribbon mics
Mid-side recording
Blumlein technique
Frequency Response
(Pale Teal) 		Resonant Frequency
(5.6) f_res = 1/(2π√(M_d × C_d))
Diaphragm Resonance:
• M_d: Effective diaphragm mass
• C_d: Diaphragm compliance
• Target: Above audio range (>20kHz)
• Effects: Peak in response, reduced linearity
Primary resonance of diaphragm-suspension system. Determines upper frequency limit and response smoothness. Target f_res:
>20kHz for
flat audio response
Proximity Effect
(5.14) Boost(f,d) = 20log₁₀[1 + (λ/2πd)²]^0.5
Proximity Effect Characteristics:
• Cause: Pressure gradient operation near sources
• Effect: Low-frequency boost with close sources
• Distance dependent: Stronger at closer distances
• Mitigation: High-pass filtering, proper technique
Low-frequency boost in pressure-gradient microphones when used close to sources. Inherent characteristic affecting tonal balance. Vocal "warmth"
Can be excessive
Use high-pass filter
Off-Axis Response
(5.15) H(f,θ) = H(f,0°) × D(θ,f)
Off-Axis Considerations:
• Frequency dependent: Pattern changes with frequency
• Coloration: Off-axis sounds have different timbre
• Design goal: Consistent off-axis response
• Applications: Critical for stereo imaging
Frequency response varies with angle of incidence. Important for stereo imaging and sound coloration in reverberant fields. High freq.
more directional
affects room sound
Performance Specs
(Pale Orange) 		Signal-to-Noise Ratio
(5.16) SNR = SPL_ref - SPL_noise (dB)
SNR Measurement:
• Reference: 1 Pa (94 dB SPL) input
• A-weighted noise: Human hearing weighted
• Typical values: 70-80 dB for good mics
• Thermal noise: Fundamental limit
Ratio of signal level to inherent noise floor. Critical specification for low-level recording applications. Good condenser:
76 dB SNR
A-weighted
Maximum SPL
(5.17) SPL_max = 20log₁₀(V_max/M) + 94 dB
SPL Limitations:
• Condenser: Limited by bias voltage and gap
• Dynamic: Limited by magnetic saturation
• Ribbon: Limited by ribbon displacement
• Typical range: 120-140 dB SPL
Maximum sound pressure level before distortion exceeds specified limit (typically 1% THD). Critical for high-SPL applications. Rock concert:
120+ dB SPL
required
Dynamic Range
(5.18) DR = SPL_max - SPL_noise (dB)
Dynamic Range Applications:
• Classical music: Requires >100 dB DR
• Studio recording: 110+ dB preferred
• Live sound: 90-100 dB adequate
• Broadcast: Limited by transmission
Usable range between noise floor and maximum SPL. Determines suitability for demanding recording applications. High-end condensers:
120+ dB
dynamic range
Equivalent Noise Level
(5.19) EIN = 94 - SNR (dB SPL, A-weighted)
Noise Sources:
• Thermal noise: Johnson noise in resistive elements
• Electronic noise: Preamp and bias circuits
• Mechanical noise: Diaphragm vibration
• Environmental: Air conditioning, electrical
Equivalent input noise level referenced to standard acoustic input. Alternative specification format for noise performance. Quiet studio:
14 dB SPL EIN
excellent

Shotgun Design:

Highly directional microphones using interference tube to enhance directivity. Length determines low-frequency directional limit. Film production

Environmental Sensitivity:

Environmental factors affecting microphone performance. Critical for measurement applications and outdoor recording. Condenser drift:

Microphone Selection Guidelines & Applications

Application-Specific Recommendations:

  • Vocal Recording: Large-diaphragm condenser, cardioid pattern, pop filter, controlled environment
  • Live Performance: Dynamic microphone, cardioid pattern, high SPL capability, feedback rejection
  • Acoustic Instruments: Small-diaphragm condenser, matched pairs for stereo, low noise
  • Measurement: Precision condenser, omnidirectional, traceable calibration, environmental stability

Key Specifications: Sensitivity: -60 to -20 dB re 1V/Pa | SNR: >70 dB A-weighted | Max SPL: >120 dB | Impedance: 150-200Ω balanced
