Signal Quality Signal-to-Noise Ratio
SNR (dB)		 SNR_dB = 20 log₁₀(V_signal / V_noise) Primary measure of recording system quality. Higher values indicate cleaner signal reproduction with less background noise contamination. Professional: >90dB
Consumer: >70dB
Total Harmonic Distortion
THD (%)		 THD = (Σ V_harmonics² / V_fundamental²) × 100% Measures non-linear distortion in reproduction chain. Lower values indicate more faithful signal reproduction without unwanted harmonics. Excellent: <0.1%
Good: <1%
Poor: >3%
Signal-to-Distortion Ratio
SDR (dB)		 SDR_dB = 20 log₁₀(1 / THD_ratio) Alternative expression of THD in decibel form. Useful for comparing distortion performance across different systems. THD 0.1% = 60dB SDR
Crosstalk Isolation
Isolation (dB)		 Isolation_dB = 20 log₁₀(V_desired / V_crosstalk) Measures unwanted signal leakage between channels in stereo/multichannel systems. Critical for spatial audio reproduction. Good: >60dB
Excellent: >80dB
Digital Audio Nyquist Sampling Theorem
f_s (Hz)		 f_s ≥ 2 f_max Fundamental requirement for digital audio conversion. Sampling rate must be at least twice the highest frequency to avoid aliasing distortion. 44.1kHz for 20kHz audio
96kHz for wide-band
Digital Bitrate
Bitrate (bps)		 Bitrate = f_s × N_bits × N_channels Data rate requirement for uncompressed digital audio. Determines storage and transmission bandwidth requirements. CD quality: 1.4Mbps
24/96: 4.6Mbps
Dynamic Range
DR (dB)		 DR ≈ 6.02 N_bits + 1.76 dB Theoretical maximum dynamic range of PCM digital system. Each bit provides approximately 6dB of dynamic range. 16-bit: 96dB
24-bit: 144dB
Quantization Noise Floor
Noise (dBFS)		 Noise_floor = -6.02 N_bits - 1.76 dB Minimum noise level in ideal PCM system due to quantization error. Sets theoretical limit for signal-to-noise ratio. 16-bit: -98dBFS
24-bit: -146dBFS
Oversampling Factor
OSR (ratio)		 OSR = f_s_actual / (2 × f_max) Ratio of actual sampling rate to Nyquist minimum. Higher oversampling reduces anti-aliasing filter requirements and improves performance. Typical: 2-8x
Delta-Sigma: 64-256x
Power & Amplification RMS Power
P (W)		 P = V² / R = I² × R Continuous power delivered to speaker load. Primary specification for amplifier-speaker matching and thermal considerations. Home: 20-100W
Pro: 200-2000W
Peak Power
P_peak (W)		 P_peak = 2 × P_RMS Maximum instantaneous power for sinusoidal signals. Used for amplifier headroom calculations and speaker protection system design. Peak = 2× RMS for sine waves
Power Efficiency
η (%)		 η = (P_out / P_in) × 100% Ratio of useful audio power output to total electrical power consumption. Critical for portable devices and large-scale installations. Class A: 25%
Class D: >90%
Damping Factor
DF (ratio)		 DF = R_load / R_source Measure of amplifier's ability to control speaker motion. Higher values provide tighter bass response and better transient control. Good: >50
Excellent: >200
Load Matching
Z (Ω)		 P_max when R_load = R_source Maximum power transfer occurs when load impedance equals source impedance. However, voltage matching (high source impedance) preferred for audio. Typical speaker: 4-16Ω
Amp output: <0.1Ω
Headroom
Headroom (dB)		 Headroom = 20 log₁₀(V_max / V_nominal) Available amplifier power above nominal operating level. Prevents clipping on transient peaks and maintains audio quality. Minimum: 6dB
Professional: 12-20dB
Frequency Response Anti-Aliasing Filter
f_c (Hz)		 f_c ≤ f_s / 2 Low-pass filter before ADC to prevent aliasing. Cutoff frequency must be below Nyquist frequency with sufficient rolloff rate. Typically f_s/2.56
Steep rolloff required
Reconstruction Filter
f_c (Hz)		 f_c = f_s / 2 Low-pass filter after DAC to remove sampling artifacts. Smooths staircase DAC output back to continuous analog signal. Same as Nyquist freq
Smoother rolloff OK
Filter Order vs Rolloff
Rolloff (dB/octave)		 Rolloff = n × 6 dB/octave Higher order filters provide steeper rolloff rates. Each additional pole adds 6dB/octave to the asymptotic rolloff rate. 2nd order: 12dB/oct
8th order: 48dB/oct
Group Delay
τ_g (s)		 τ_g = -dφ/dω Frequency-dependent time delay through filter. Constant group delay preserves waveform shape; variable delay causes phase distortion. Linear phase preferred
Bessel filters optimal
Frequency Response Tolerance
Tolerance (dB)		 ±1 dB (20Hz - 20kHz) Acceptable variation in magnitude response across audio frequency range. Tighter tolerance provides more neutral sound reproduction. Hi-Fi: ±1dB
Professional: ±0.5dB
Recording Systems Microphone Sensitivity
S (mV/Pa)		 S_dBV = 20 log₁₀(V_out / V_ref) Output voltage per unit sound pressure. Determines required preamp gain and system noise performance for given sound pressure levels. Condenser: -40dBV
Dynamic: -55dBV
Preamp Gain Requirement
Gain (dB)		 Gain = 20 log₁₀(V_line / V_mic) Amplification needed to bring microphone signal up to line level. Must provide sufficient gain while maintaining low noise figure. Typical: 40-60dB
Quiet sources: 70dB+
Equivalent Input Noise
EIN (dBu)		 EIN = Noise_out - Gain Input noise level referred to preamp input. Lower values indicate better noise performance and greater usable dynamic range. Good: -120dBu
Excellent: -128dBu
Recording Level
Level (dBFS)		 Peak level ≈ -6 to -12 dBFS Optimal digital recording levels to maximize SNR while avoiding clipping. Provides headroom for unexpected peaks and post-processing. Conservative: -12dBFS
Aggressive: -6dBFS
Reproduction Speaker Sensitivity
SPL (dB @ 1W/1m)		 SPL = SPL_ref + 10 log₁₀(P/P_ref) Sound pressure level produced by 1 watt input at 1 meter distance. Higher sensitivity requires less amplifier power for given SPL. Bookshelf: 85-88dB
Horn: 100+ dB
Maximum SPL
SPL_max (dB)		 SPL_max = Sensitivity + 10 log₁₀(P_max) Peak sound pressure level capability of speaker system. Limited by thermal power handling and mechanical excursion limits. Home: 105dB
Pro monitor: 120dB+
Listening Distance
Distance (m)		 SPL_listener = SPL_1m - 20 log₁₀(distance) Sound pressure level decreases 6dB for each doubling of distance in free field. Room acoustics modify this relationship at low frequencies. Near-field: 0.5-1.5m
Far-field: 2-4m
Power Compression
Compression (dB)		 Compression = SPL_predicted - SPL_actual Reduction in output due to voice coil heating at high power levels. Affects dynamic range and requires thermal power derating. Acceptable: <3dB
Problematic: >6dB

Key Design Considerations for Recording Systems

Signal Chain Optimization: Each stage in the recording chain (microphone → preamp → ADC → storage) contributes noise and distortion. The microphone preamp typically dominates noise performance, so high-quality, low-noise preamps are essential. Choose preamps with EIN below -120dBu for professional applications.

Digital Resolution Selection: While 24-bit recording provides 144dB theoretical dynamic range, real-world performance is limited by analog electronics to approximately 120-130dB. However, the extra headroom prevents quantization distortion during processing and provides margin for gain adjustments in post-production.

Sample Rate Considerations: 44.1kHz is adequate for consumer playback, but higher rates (96kHz, 192kHz) provide benefits during recording and processing: reduced anti-aliasing filter phase distortion, better performance with nonlinear processing, and margin for pitch shifting without aliasing.

Reproduction System Design Guidelines

Amplifier Selection: Choose amplifiers with power ratings 2-4 times the speaker's nominal power handling. This ensures adequate headroom for transient peaks and prevents clipping distortion. High damping factor (>100) provides better bass control.

Room Acoustics Integration: Speaker placement and room treatment significantly affect frequency response. Position monitors to minimize early reflections and consider acoustic treatment for first reflection points. Near-field monitors reduce room interaction effects.

Level Calibration: Establish consistent monitoring levels using pink noise and SPL meter. Typical professional levels: 85dB SPL for mix position, with capability for 105dB peak levels. This provides adequate dynamic range while protecting hearing.

Practical Design Example - Home Studio Setup:
• Microphone: Condenser, -37dBV sensitivity
• Preamp: 60dB max gain, -125dBu EIN
• Interface: 24-bit/96kHz ADC
• Monitors: 88dB sensitivity, 100W power
• Amplifier: 150W/channel, >200 damping factor
• Expected performance: >115dB dynamic range, <0.01% THD
