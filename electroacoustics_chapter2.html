Chapter 2 – Equalizers and Frequency Response

Comprehensive reference for filter responses, crossover networks, and frequency-dependent gain curves used in audio engineering and sound measurement.

Section Concept Formula/Rule Explanation/Use Case Notes
Basic Filter Responses
(Pale Blue) 		Low-pass Filter
(2.1) H_lowpass(ω) = 1 / √[1 + (ω/ωc)²]
Key Properties:
• -3dB at cutoff frequency ωc
• -20dB/decade rolloff above ωc
• Passes low frequencies, attenuates high
Magnitude response of first-order low-pass filter. Used for anti-aliasing, noise reduction, and treble control. At ωc: H = 0.707 (-3dB)
High-pass Filter
(2.2) H_highpass(ω) = (ω/ωc) / √[1 + (ω/ωc)²]
Key Properties:
• -3dB at cutoff frequency ωc
• +20dB/decade rolloff below ωc
• Passes high frequencies, attenuates low
Magnitude response of first-order high-pass filter. Used for removing DC offset, rumble filtering, and bass control. At ωc: H = 0.707 (-3dB)
Band-pass Filter
(2.3) H_bandpass(ω) = (ω/ω₀) / √[(1-(ω/ω₀)²)² + (ω/ω₀)²/Q²]
Corrected:
H_bandpass(ω) = (ω/ω₀)/Q / √[(1-(ω/ω₀)²)² + (ω/ω₀)²/Q²]
Key Properties:
• Peak at center frequency ω₀
• Q determines bandwidth: BW = ω₀/Q
• Higher Q = narrower bandwidth
Magnitude response of second-order band-pass filter. Used for tone controls, graphic EQ bands, and frequency analysis. Q=10: narrow band
Q=1: wide band
Gain Control
(Pale Green) 		EQ Stage Gain
(2.4) G_dB = 20 log₁₀(Vout/Vin)
Linear to dB Conversion:
• 2× voltage = +6.02 dB
• 10× voltage = +20 dB
• 0.5× voltage = -6.02 dB
Converts voltage gain ratio to decibel representation. Essential for characterizing amplifier and EQ stage performance. Unity gain = 0 dB
Passive Filter Design
(Pale Pink) 		RC Filter Cutoff
(2.5) fc = 1 / (2πRC)
Design Guidelines:
• Larger RC = lower cutoff frequency
• For fc = 1kHz: R=1.6kΩ, C=100nF
• RC time constant τ = RC
Cutoff frequency of simple RC low-pass or high-pass filter. Fundamental building block for passive filters. R=1kΩ, C=100nF → fc=1.59kHz
LC Crossover Network
(2.6) f_crossover = 1 / (2π√(LC))
Speaker Crossover Design:
• Separates frequency bands for different drivers
• Typically 2-way: woofer + tweeter
• Common crossover: 2-3 kHz
Resonant frequency of LC circuit used in speaker crossover networks. Determines transition between drivers. L=1mH, C=100nF → fc=15.9kHz
Standardized Weighting
(Pale Teal) 		A-Weighting
(2.7a) Frequency-dependent gain curve
A-Weighting Characteristics:
• Mimics human hearing at low levels (40 phons)
• -30dB at 100Hz, -10dB at 1kHz
• Peak around 2-5kHz
• Most common for noise measurement
Approximates human hearing sensitivity at low sound levels. Used for environmental noise assessment and audio equipment specs. dB(A) units
B-Weighting
(2.7b) Frequency-dependent gain curve
B-Weighting Characteristics:
• Designed for intermediate levels (70 phons)
• Less low-frequency attenuation than A
• Rarely used in modern practice
• Between A and C weighting curves
Intermediate weighting curve for moderate sound levels. Historically used but largely replaced by A-weighting. dB(B) units
(obsolete)
C-Weighting
(2.7c) Frequency-dependent gain curve
C-Weighting Characteristics:
• Nearly flat response 100Hz-10kHz
• Approximates hearing at high levels (100 phons)
• Used for peak measurements
• Good for impulsive noise assessment
Nearly flat frequency response for high sound levels. Used for peak sound level measurements and impulse noise. dB(C) units
Z-Weighting
(2.7d) No frequency weighting (flat)
Z-Weighting (Linear):
• Completely flat frequency response
• No frequency-dependent attenuation
• Used for analysis and calibration
• Replaced "Linear" designation
No frequency weighting applied - flat response. Used for technical analysis and equipment calibration. dB(Z) or dB(Lin)
Filter Characteristics
(Pale Orange) 		Quality Factor (Q)
(2.8) Q = f₀ / BW = f₀ / (f₂ - f₁)
Q Factor Interpretation:
• Higher Q = sharper, more selective filter
• Lower Q = broader, more gentle filter
• Q = 0.707 for Butterworth response
• Typical EQ: Q = 1-10
Measure of filter selectivity. Ratio of center frequency to -3dB bandwidth. Critical for EQ design. Q=10: very narrow
Q=1: moderate
Q=0.5: wide
Angular Frequency
(2.9) ω = 2πf
Frequency Conversion:
• ω in radians per second (rad/s)
• f in hertz (Hz or cycles/s)
• Useful for mathematical analysis
• Standard in filter design equations
Conversion between frequency in Hz and angular frequency in rad/s. Essential for filter analysis and design. 1kHz = 6283 rad/s

🔧 Design Guidelines & Quick Reference

Common Filter Applications:

  • Low-pass: Anti-aliasing (fs/2), treble control, noise reduction
  • High-pass: DC blocking (20Hz), rumble filter, bass control
  • Band-pass: Graphic EQ bands, tone controls, analysis filters

Typical Values: RC filters: 100Ω-10kΩ, 10nF-1µF | Crossovers: 2-4kHz | EQ bands: 31Hz-16kHz

🎯 Study & Application Guidelines

  1. Master Filter Basics: Understand the -3dB point and rolloff rates for each filter type
  2. Practice Component Design: Use equations 2.5 and 2.6 to design filters for specific frequencies
  3. Understand Q Factor: Learn how Q affects filter selectivity and bandwidth
  4. Apply Weighting Curves: Know when to use A, C, or Z weighting for different measurement purposes
  5. Design Considerations: Consider component tolerances, temperature drift, and loading effects
