Chapter 4 – Electrical, Mechanical & Acoustic Analogies

Comprehensive reference for analogous relationships between electrical, mechanical, and acoustic systems, enabling unified analysis and design approaches across domains.

Section Concept Formula/Rule Explanation/Use Case Notes
Electrical Domain
(Pale Blue) 		Resistive Impedance
(4.1) Z_R = R
Resistance Properties:
• Frequency independent
• Energy dissipation (heat)
• In-phase voltage/current
• Real impedance component
Pure resistance shows no frequency dependence. Fundamental electrical element for energy dissipation. 8Ω speaker
1kΩ resistor
Inductive Impedance
(4.2) Z_L = jωL
Inductance Properties:
• Frequency dependent: |Z_L| = ωL
• Energy storage (magnetic field)
• Voltage leads current by 90°
• Opposes current changes
Inductive reactance increases with frequency. Models magnetic energy storage and current opposition. 1mH @ 1kHz:
|Z_L| = 6.28Ω
Capacitive Impedance
(4.3) Z_C = 1/(jωC)
Capacitance Properties:
• Frequency dependent: |Z_C| = 1/(ωC)
• Energy storage (electric field)
• Current leads voltage by 90°
• Opposes voltage changes
Capacitive reactance decreases with frequency. Models electric energy storage and voltage opposition. 100µF @ 1kHz:
|Z_C| = 1.59Ω
Ohm's Law
(4.1a) V = I × Z
Electrical Domain Variables:
• Across variable: Voltage (V)
• Through variable: Current (I)
• Impedance: Z = V/I
Fundamental relationship between voltage, current, and impedance. Basis for all electrical analogies. 12V / 8Ω
= 1.5A
Mechanical Domain
(Pale Green) 		Newton's Second Law
(4.4) F = M × a = M × (dv/dt)
Mass Properties:
• Analogous to electrical inductance
• Opposes acceleration (velocity changes)
• Energy storage (kinetic energy)
• Z_mass = jωM
Force required to accelerate mass. Mechanical analogy to inductance - opposes velocity changes. 0.1kg mass
10m/s² accel
F = 1N
Velocity Definition
(4.5) v = dx/dt
Mechanical Velocity Analogy:
• Electrical: Current (I) - charge flow rate
• Mechanical: Velocity (v) - displacement rate
• Both are "through" variables
Rate of displacement change. Mechanical through variable analogous to electrical current. 10mm/s
= 0.01 m/s
Spring Compliance
(4.5a) F = x/K or Z_spring = 1/(jωK)
Compliance Properties:
• Analogous to electrical capacitance
• K = compliance (m/N)
• Opposes displacement
• Energy storage (potential energy)
Spring compliance relates force to displacement. Mechanical analogy to capacitance. Stiff spring:
low K
Soft spring:
high K
Mechanical Resistance
(4.5b) F = R_mech × v
Mechanical Resistance:
• Analogous to electrical resistance
• Energy dissipation (friction, damping)
• Frequency independent
• Opposes motion
Mechanical damping or friction. Direct analogy to electrical resistance with energy dissipation. Viscous damping
Air resistance
Friction
Mechanical Impedance
(4.5c) Z_mech = F/v
Mechanical Domain Variables:
• Across variable: Force (F)
• Through variable: Velocity (v)
• Impedance: Z_mech = F/v
Ratio of force to velocity. Mechanical impedance analogous to electrical impedance. High Z: hard to move
Low Z: easy to move
Acoustic Domain
(Pale Pink) 		Pressure Definition
(4.6) p = F/A
Acoustic Pressure Analogy:
• Electrical: Voltage (V) - potential difference
• Mechanical: Force (F) - mechanical effort
• Acoustic: Pressure (p) - acoustic potential
Force per unit area. Acoustic across variable analogous to electrical voltage and mechanical force. 10N over 0.01m²
= 1000 Pa
Volume Velocity
(4.7) U = v × A
Volume Velocity Analogy:
• Electrical: Current (I) - charge flow
• Mechanical: Velocity (v) - motion rate
• Acoustic: Volume velocity (U) - volume flow rate
Volume flow rate through acoustic system. Acoustic through variable analogous to current and velocity. 1mm/s × 0.01m²
= 10mm³/s
Acoustic Impedance
(4.8) Z_acoustic = p/U
Acoustic Impedance Types:
• Specific: Z_s = p/v (Pa·s/m)
• Mechanical: Z_a = p/U (Pa·s/m³)
• Characteristic: Z₀ = ρc (air ≈ 415 rayls)
• Frequency dependent for reactive elements
Ratio of acoustic pressure to volume velocity. Fundamental acoustic impedance definition. Air: Z₀ ≈ 415
Water: Z₀ ≈ 1.5×10⁶
(rayls)
Acoustic Elements
(4.8a) Z_mass = jωM_a, Z_comp = 1/(jωC_a), Z_resist = R_a
Acoustic Element Analogies:
• Mass: M_a = ρL/A (air column)
• Compliance: C_a = V/(ρc²) (cavity)
• Resistance: R_a (friction, radiation)
• Same impedance forms as electrical
Acoustic mass, compliance, and resistance follow same impedance relationships as electrical elements. Port: acoustic mass
Cavity: compliance
Damping: resistance
Resonant Systems
(Pale Teal) 		Helmholtz Resonator
(4.9) f_H = (c/2π) × √[S/(V×L)]
Helmholtz Parameters:
• c: Speed of sound (343 m/s @ 20°C)
• S: Neck cross-sectional area
• V: Cavity volume
• L: Effective neck length (L + ΔL)
• ΔL ≈ 0.85√(S/π): End correction
Resonant frequency of cavity with neck opening. Acoustic LC resonator with mass (neck) and compliance (cavity). 1cm² neck
1L volume
1cm length
≈ 546 Hz
Resonance Condition
(4.9a) ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = √(1/(M_a×C_a))
Universal Resonance:
• Electrical: ω₀ = 1/√(LC)
• Mechanical: ω₀ = √(1/(MK))
• Acoustic: ω₀ = √(1/(M_a C_a))
• All domains follow same mathematical form
Universal resonance condition across all three domains. Inertial and compliance elements determine resonant frequency. Minimum impedance
Maximum response
at resonance

Conversion Factors
(Pale Purple) 		Acoustic Mass
(4.10) M_a = ρL/A
Acoustic Mass Applications:
• Air columns: Speaker ports, tubes
• ρ: Air density (1.21 kg/m³ @ 20°C)
• L: Effective length (includes end corrections)
• A: Cross-sectional area
Acoustic mass of air column in tube or port. Represents inertia of oscillating air mass. 5cm port
1cm² area:
M_a = 6.05×10⁻⁵ kg/m⁴
Acoustic Compliance
(4.11) C_a = V/(ρc²)
Acoustic Compliance Applications:
• Cavities: Speaker enclosures, rooms
• V: Volume of air space
• ρc²: Bulk modulus of air (≈ 1.42×10⁵ Pa)
• Represents air "springiness"
Acoustic compliance of enclosed air volume. Represents compressibility of trapped air. 1L enclosure:
C_a = 7.04×10⁻⁹ m⁵/N
Radiation Impedance
(4.12) Z_rad = ρc/A × [R₁(ka) + jX₁(ka)]
Radiation Impedance:
• ka: Wavenumber × radius
• R₁, X₁: Radiation resistance and reactance functions
• Low frequency: Z_rad ≈ jωρc(ka)²/2
• High frequency: Z_rad ≈ ρc
Impedance seen by vibrating surface radiating into free space. Frequency-dependent load on acoustic sources. Large piston:
Z_rad → ρc
Small piston:
Z_rad → jωM_rad

🔧 Analogy Applications & Design Guidelines

Common Applications:

  • Speaker Design: Thiele-Small parameters use electrical analogies for mechanical/acoustic elements
  • Room Acoustics: Model rooms as acoustic networks with mass, compliance, and resistance
  • Filter Design: Translate electrical filter theory to mechanical and acoustic systems
  • Transducer Analysis: Unified models spanning electrical input to acoustic output

Key Constants: Air density ρ = 1.21 kg/m³ | Speed of sound c = 343 m/s | Characteristic impedance Z₀ = ρc ≈ 415 rayls (@ 20°C)
