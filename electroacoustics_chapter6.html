ELECTROACOUSTICS

REFERENCE SHEET

Chapter 6 – Loudspeakers and Electroacoustic Transduction

Comprehensive reference for loudspeaker design, analysis, and performance covering electromagnetic transduction, mechanical systems, acoustic radiation, and enclosure design.

Section Concept Formula/Rule Explanation/Use Case Notes
Electromagnetic Transduction
(Pale Blue) 		Lorentz Force
(6.1) F = B × l × i
Voice Coil Parameters:
• B: Magnetic flux density in gap (0.5-2.0 T)
• l: Effective conductor length (1-5 cm)
• i: Instantaneous current
• Bl: Force factor (motor strength)
Force generated by current-carrying conductor in magnetic field. Fundamental electromagnetic-to-mechanical conversion in loudspeakers. B=1T, l=2cm
i=1A → F=0.02N
Back EMF
(6.1a) e = B × l × v
Electromagnetic Coupling:
• e: Induced voltage (back EMF)
• v: Diaphragm velocity
• Same Bl factor for both directions
• Limits maximum SPL and damping
Voltage induced by moving conductor in magnetic field. Provides electromagnetic damping and couples mechanical motion to electrical circuit. Moving coil acts
as generator
(damping effect)
Force Factor Quality
(6.1b) Bl = √(P_e × R_e × Z_mech) (at resonance)
Force Factor Optimization:
• Higher Bl = more efficient conversion
• Limited by magnetic saturation
• Trade-off with voice coil mass
• Affects sensitivity and power handling
Relationship between electrical power, mechanical impedance, and electromagnetic coupling strength. Typical Bl:
5-20 T·m
for drivers
Mechanical System
(Pale Green) 		Displacement Response
(6.2) x(ω) = F(ω) / Z_mech(ω)
Frequency Response Shaping:
• Below resonance: compliance controlled
• At resonance: resistance controlled
• Above resonance: mass controlled
• Determines driver frequency response
Diaphragm displacement as function of driving force and mechanical impedance. Determines frequency response characteristics. Large displacement
at low frequencies
(below f_res)
Mechanical Impedance
(6.3) Z_mech = R_mech + jωM + 1/(jωC)
Thiele-Small Parameters:
• M_ms: Moving mass (cone + voice coil)
• C_ms: Suspension compliance
• R_ms: Mechanical resistance (damping)
• Determines resonance and Q-factor
Complex mechanical impedance of moving system. Combines mass, compliance, and resistance effects. At resonance:
Z_mech = R_mech
(minimum)
Resonant Frequency
(6.7) f_res = f_s = 1/(2π√(M_ms × C_ms))
Resonance Effects:
• Free-air resonance (f_s): Driver alone
• System resonance (f_c): In enclosure
• Peak in impedance and response
• Critical for crossover design
Natural resonant frequency of mechanical system. Determines low-frequency rolloff and enclosure tuning requirements. Woofer: 20-100Hz
Midrange: 100-500Hz
Tweeter: 500-2kHz
Quality Factor
(6.3a) Q_ms = (2πf_s × M_ms)/R_ms = √(M_ms/C_ms)/R_ms
Q Factor Effects:
• High Q (>1): Underdamped, peaky response
• Q ≈ 0.7: Critically damped, smooth response
• Low Q (<0.5): Overdamped, rolled-off response
• Controls transient response
Mechanical quality factor determining resonance sharpness and damping characteristics. Typical Q_ms:
1-10
for drivers
Acoustic Radiation
(Pale Pink) 		Volume Velocity
(6.4) U = v × S_d
Effective Radiating Area:
• S_d: Effective diaphragm area
• Usually less than physical area
• Accounts for surround and suspension
• Critical for acoustic output calculation
Volume velocity of acoustic radiation from diaphragm. Links mechanical motion to acoustic output. v=1mm/s
S_d=100cm²
U=10mm³/s
Point Source Pressure
(6.5) p = ρcU/(4πr²) × jωr/c (far-field)
Radiation Characteristics:
• Near-field (r < λ/2π): Reactive components
• Far-field (r > λ/2π): Real radiation
• Directivity: Frequency dependent
• Pressure ∝ 1/r in far-field
Sound pressure at distance from point source approximation. Valid for compact sources in far-field. Pressure drops
6dB per
distance doubling
Acoustic Power
(6.5a) P_acoustic = Re[p × U*]/2 = ρcU²/(4πr²) × (ωr/c)²
Power Radiation:
• Low frequency: P ∝ ω⁴ (mass law)
• High frequency: P ∝ U² (area law)
• Radiation resistance: R_r = P_acoustic/U²
• Fundamental efficiency limitation
Acoustic power radiated by source. Determines acoustic efficiency and frequency response shape. Low freq: very
inefficient
(ω⁴ law)
Sensitivity
(6.5b) SPL = 20log₁₀(p/p_ref) = Sensitivity + 10log₁₀(P_e) - 20log₁₀(r)
Sensitivity Specification:
• Measured at 1m with 1W input
• Typical range: 80-110 dB SPL/W/m
• Higher sensitivity = more efficient
• Trade-off with bandwidth and size
Sound pressure level produced by given electrical power at specified distance. Key specification for loudspeaker efficiency. Typical sensitivity:
85-95 dB SPL
@ 1W/1m
Efficiency & Performance
(Pale Teal) 		Electroacoustic Efficiency
(6.6) η = P_acoustic/P_electric
Efficiency Factors:
• Electromagnetic: Bl²/(R_e × Z_mech)
• Mechano-acoustic: R_radiation/Z_mech
• Overall: Product of all conversions
• Typically 0.1-10% for direct radiators
Ratio of useful acoustic power output to electrical power input. Fundamental measure of loudspeaker efficiency. Typical η:
0.5-5%
Most energy → heat
Maximum SPL
(6.6a) SPL_max = Sensitivity + 10log₁₀(P_max) - 20log₁₀(r)
Power Limitations:
• Thermal: Voice coil heating (RMS power)
• Mechanical: Excursion limits (peak power)
• Magnetic: Nonlinearity and distortion
• Frequency dependent limitations
Maximum sound pressure level achievable before damage or excessive distortion. Limited by thermal and mechanical constraints. Professional drivers:
130+ dB SPL
@ 1m possible
Frequency Response
(6.6b) H(ω) = [Bl/(R_e + jωL_e)] × [jω/(Z_mech)] × [Z_radiation]
Response Shaping:
• Electrical: Voice coil inductance rolloff
• Mechanical: Resonance and mass control
• Acoustic: Radiation characteristics
• Enclosure: Loading and resonances
Complete electroacoustic transfer function from electrical input to acoustic output. Combines all system elements. Flat response
requires careful
system design
Thiele-Small Parameters
(Pale Orange) 		Electrical Q
(6.8) Q_es = (2πf_s × M_ms × R_e)/(Bl)²
Q_es Significance:
• Electrical damping at resonance
• Lower Q_es = more electromagnetic damping
• Affects system Q and enclosure design
• Typical range: 0.2-1.0
Electrical quality factor representing electromagnetic damping. Determines electrical contribution to total system damping. Lower Q_es:
tighter bass
response
Total Q
(6.9) Q_ts = (Q_ms × Q_es)/(Q_ms + Q_es)
Q_ts Applications:
• Determines enclosure suitability
• Q_ts < 0.4: Sealed box
• Q_ts > 0.4: Ported box
• Controls transient response
Total system Q-factor combining mechanical and electrical damping. Critical parameter for enclosure design selection. Q_ts = 0.3-0.7
for most
applications
Equivalent Volume
(6.10) V_as = ρc² × S_d² × C_ms
V_as Interpretation:
• Volume of air with same compliance as suspension
• Determines required enclosure size
• Larger V_as = stiffer suspension
• Scaled with driver size and compliance
Equivalent air compliance volume. Represents suspension stiffness and determines minimum enclosure size requirements. Typical V_as:
10-200L
